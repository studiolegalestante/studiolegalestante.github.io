Flight delayed, cancelled or overbooked: compensation up to €600

If your flight arrived at least three hours late, was cancelled or you were denied boarding, the airline usually owes you €250 to €600 per passenger under EU law, on top of a refund or re-routing. Calculate here the amount provided for.

What happened?

When compensation is due

  • Delay of at least three hours on arrival at your final destination;
  • cancellation notified less than 14 days before departure, unless the airline offers an alternative flight with times very close to the original ones;
  • denied boarding against your will, for example because of overbooking.

The EU rules cover every flight departing from an EU airport, with any airline, and flights arriving in the EU operated by a European airline. The amount depends on distance: €250 up to 1,500 km, €400 for intra-EU flights over 1,500 km and other flights between 1,500 and 3,500 km, €600 beyond that.

The airline does not pay if it proves extraordinary circumstances, such as bad weather, airspace closure or an air traffic control strike. Ordinary technical faults and strikes by the airline's own staff do not count.

Other rights, always due

  • Refund or re-routing in case of cancellation or denied boarding, and a refund if the delay exceeds five hours and you decide not to travel;
  • care while you wait: meals and drinks and, if needed, a hotel and transport to it;
  • documented expenses caused by the disruption, such as a hotel night or an excursion already paid and lost.

Lost, damaged or delayed baggage

Report it immediately at the airport baggage desk, before leaving, by filling in the property irregularity report. Then send a written complaint to the airline: within 7 days for damaged baggage, within 21 days of delivery for delayed baggage.

What changes from 23 October 2027

The new EU regulation on air passenger rights, published in October 2026, will apply from 23 October 2027. Compensation amounts stay the same, but the claim will have to be sent to the airline within nine months of the flight.

Frequently asked questions

My flight landed 3.5 hours late: am I owed anything?
Usually yes: €250 to €600 per passenger depending on distance, unless the airline proves extraordinary circumstances. What counts is the time the aircraft doors open at the destination, not the landing time.
The airline blames a technical fault: does it still have to pay?
As a rule, yes. Faults that arise in the normal course of maintenance are part of the airline's business risk and do not exempt it from paying compensation.
They offered me a voucher: do I have to accept it?
No. Refunds are due in money; you can accept a voucher only if you choose to, by signed agreement.
I missed my connecting flight: what counts?
If both flights were on a single booking, what counts is the delay at your final destination, and the distance is measured from the first airport to the last.
How long do I have to claim?
Under the current rules, in Italy it is advisable to act within two years of the flight. From 23 October 2027 the claim must be sent to the airline within nine months.
I live abroad: can you handle my claim against an airline from Bari?
Yes. Everything can be done online: you send the documents by email, I send the formal claim and, if needed, start proceedings before the competent Italian court.
