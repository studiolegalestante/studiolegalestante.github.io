Personal injury compensation in Italy: accidents, falls, injuries at work

I assist people injured in Italy, including foreign residents and tourists: road accidents, falls on damaged pavements, accidents at work, insurance claims. With a trusted forensic doctor I assess the injuries and quantify the documented heads of loss before assessing the insurer's offer.

What I deal with

  • Road accidents: drivers, passengers, pedestrians, cyclists and e-scooter riders;
  • falls and hazards: potholes, manholes, damaged pavements, wet floors in shops and supermarkets, stairs in apartment buildings;
  • accidents at work: beyond the INAIL benefit (the Italian workplace injury insurer), the further damage owed by an employer who breached safety rules;
  • accident and insurance policies: when the insurer refuses to pay or offers less than is due.

Road accidents in Italy

The claim is sent to the insurer with the accident documents. The insurer must make an offer or give reasons for refusing within 60 days where only vehicles are damaged, 30 days if both drivers signed the joint accident report, and 90 days where there are injuries.

  • A passenger claims from the insurer of the vehicle they were travelling in, whatever the drivers' responsibility.
  • If the vehicle at fault was uninsured or not identified, the Italian Guarantee Fund for road victims steps in.
  • Before going to court, assisted negotiation between lawyers is mandatory.

How injury compensation is calculated

Damage to health is measured in percentage points of permanent impairment, assessed by a forensic doctor. Minor injuries, up to 9%, follow ministerial tables updated every year; more serious ones, from 10% to 100%, follow the national single table in force for accidents from 5 March 2025, also used for medical negligence. On top of this come the days of temporary incapacity, medical expenses, loss of income and, where the consequences are particular, a personalised increase.

This is why your own forensic doctor's examination is decisive: the insurer has its own doctor, and you should have one on your side before accepting any offer.

Falls on roads, pavements and in shops

If you fall because of a pothole, a manhole or a damaged pavement, you can claim from the owner of the road, often the municipality, which is liable as custodian unless it proves an unforeseeable event. Your own care also counts: a clearly visible pothole in broad daylight may reduce or exclude compensation. The same rules apply to shops, supermarkets and apartment buildings. Gather evidence straight away: photos of the exact spot, witness names, the emergency room report.

How I work

1. Review of documents. With the online consultation I review reports, medical records, photos and correspondence with the insurer and tell you in writing whether and how much you can claim.
2. Forensic medical examination. For injuries I work with a trusted forensic doctor, who assesses the after-effects and writes the report.
3. Claim and negotiation. I send the documented claim and handle the negotiation with the insurer or the party responsible.
4. Court, if needed. If the offer is not adequate, after assisted negotiation or mediation, proceedings are brought before the Italian courts.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to claim compensation for a road accident in Italy?
As a rule, two years from the accident; the time limit is longer if the facts amount to a criminal offence, for example where there are injuries. It is best to act at once, while the evidence is fresh.
I had an accident while on holiday in Italy: can I claim from my home country?
Yes. The claim is made against the Italian insurer of the vehicle responsible, and it can be handled remotely: you send the documents and medical records, and I deal with the insurer in Italy.
The insurer has made me an offer: should I accept it?
Not before comparing it with the assessment of a forensic doctor and a lawyer. If the offer is not convincing you can take it as a payment on account, without signing a full and final settlement, and claim the difference.
I tripped on a pothole: who pays?
Usually the owner of the road, often the municipality, which is liable as custodian. You need evidence gathered straight away: photos of the exact spot, witnesses and the emergency room report.
I was a passenger: who do I claim from?
From the insurer of the vehicle you were travelling in, regardless of which driver was at fault.
